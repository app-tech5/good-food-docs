Monetization

A food marketplace only works if money flows the right way — on every order, every month, and every time a restaurant wants more visibility. This suite gives you several ways to earn, side by side, without bolting on a separate billing product.

Commissions Subscriptions Sponsored ads Wallet & payments

Earn on the order — and between orders

Most food apps stop at “take a cut of the basket.” That is useful on busy Friday nights, but it is not enough when you want a business that survives quiet Tuesday afternoons. Operators need more than a single fee: a default commission on orders, membership plans that renew, paid placement restaurants will buy, a wallet that keeps customers coming back, and payment options that match each market.

All of that lives in the same suite. You configure the rules in the admin console; customers, drivers, and restaurants see the matching screens in their apps — so monetization feels like part of the product, not a spreadsheet on the side.

Commissions that stay flexible

Set a default commission for your city, then tune it per restaurant when you need to. A new partner might need a gentler start; a high-volume kitchen can keep the standard rate. When a restaurant upgrades to a stronger subscription, you can reward them with a lower cut — a clear incentive that grows both sides of the marketplace instead of squeezing partners until they leave.

Admin earnings views show the split clearly: what the platform keeps, what the restaurant earns, ready for payouts and month-end reporting. That clarity matters when you settle with partners, pitch investors, or simply want to sleep at night.

Subscriptions that renew on their own

Offer monthly (or other) plans to three audiences at once. Customers get free delivery or member offers — a reason to open the app again this week. Drivers unlock access or priority tiers that reward the couriers who keep your SLA honest. Restaurants pay for better rates, visibility, and tools that feel worth a recurring invoice.

You create and edit tiers in admin (target role, price, billing cycle, benefits). Each app shows its own subscribe screen. Recurring revenue sits next to commission income — still paying when order volume dips.

Sponsored listings restaurants will buy

Attention on the home feed and in search is limited. Sponsored listings let restaurants compete for that spotlight with a real campaign: a headline, a daily bid, a placement (search top, home banner, or both), then a launch button. Your customer experience stays polished; partners pay for placement instead of asking for “a pin on the map” in a group chat.

From the restaurant app, partners manage their own campaigns. From admin, you keep oversight of what is live on the marketplace.

Wallet, cashback, and payments that travel

A wallet turns one-off checkout into a relationship. Customers see their balance, add money, send funds when your flow allows it, and keep a card on file under payment methods. Cashback-style rewards and smoother refunds (when you enable them) make the balance feel useful — not like a dead prepaid card.

At payment time you are not locked into a single country: marketplace-style card flows and regional gateways help you meet the habits people already trust locally.

Why this converts

Buyers evaluating food-delivery clones often ask one question after the demo: “How do I make money after launch?” Commissions, renewing plans, sponsored ads, and wallet — in the same five-product suite you host and brand — is a much stronger answer than a pretty menu browser alone.
